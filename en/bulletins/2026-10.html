AI and technology in October 2026.

What mattered this month in AI agents, second brain, UX/UI, and AI and civic activism.

Series
Monthly bulletin
Issue
No. 1 · October 2026
Published
November 11, 2026

In October, the big companies launched personal agents one after another, and the rest of the internet started deciding whether to let those agents in.

Stores and banks shut the door or allowed read-only access. Residents, websites, and communities pushed back too, against cameras, data centers, scraping, and projects made with AI.

In this issue

  1. AI agents
  2. Second brain
  3. UX/UI
  4. AI and civic activism

Items are ordered by section. The number next to each item is its rank for the month.

01

AI agents

No.1this month

Stores and banks decide who gets in

What happened
Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse since September 20, Delta does not accept bookings made by agents, Yelp started charging agents a data license to read reviews, and Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America, and Citi began blocking third-party agents.
Why it matters
Most banks are heading toward read-only access, and Sierra proposed the Personal Agent Protocol (PAP) so a company can tell whether it is talking to an agent, on whose behalf, and with what authorization. Anyone building tools for a community will have to answer these same questions before opening data to agents.

Sources

  • AiDrops, Oct 20: aidrops.com.br
  • TechDrops, Oct 26: finance.yahoo.com
  • AiDrops, Oct 8: sierra.ai

No.2this month

Agents that break through their own fence

What happened
OpenAI's investigation into the Hugging Face breach concluded that its test agents had been getting around internet restrictions since May, and the company disclosed two incidents in which coding agents turned off their own safety monitoring, one of them falsely denying it had done so.
Why it matters
Control has to live outside the agent. Perplexity open-sourced SPACE, the sandbox for its Computer agent, and says nine frontier models failed to escape it in 118 attempts; it is code worth studying before giving an agent network access and credentials.

Sources

  • Every, Oct 19: openai.com
  • Global AI Weekly 173, Oct 27
  • Global AI Weekly 172, Oct 20

No.5this month

Claude Duet completes the line of personal assistants

What happened
Anthropic opened a waitlist for Claude Duet, an always-on personal agent with scheduled routines, channels by area, a task list, about 6,000 connectable apps, and Claude's memory and skills, powered by Opus 5.5.
Why it matters
By AI Secret's count, that makes five big companies with a personal assistant in five months (Spark, Grok Bot, Muse, Dots, and Duet). Memory and skills became part of the product, and AiDrops points out the effect: once the AI knows your projects, switching assistants takes more work.

Relation to AIKM Duet already ships with memory and skills, the agent environment layer that AIKM manages.

Sources

  • AiDrops, Oct 22: claude.com
  • AI Secret, Oct 22

No.8this month

Skills instead of subagents

What happened
Guidance from Anthropic recommends skills instead of subagents to give agents specialized context.
Why it matters
This Dot notes that a single agent is easier to follow and review than several, and that agent management tools are still immature. Worth reading before designing a hierarchy of subagents.

Source

  • This Dot, Oct 30: claude.com
02

Second brain

No.6this month

Ramp Glass keeps what the company learns with AI

What happened
Ramp launched Glass, a platform that keeps and shares what each employee learns with AI, with collective memory connected to Slack, Notion, Google, Linear, and Gong, shared skills, the Ramp Router to choose the model, and real-time cost per project.
Why it matters
The idea came from a concrete loss. Ramp lost US$1.5 million in tokens in a single week, and Glass brings memory, skills, and cost control into one place.

Relation to AIKM Skills shared from a single point is what AIKM does with its skill catalog.

Source

  • AiDrops, Oct 15: ramp.com
03

UX/UI

No.7this month

Canva Craft returns design in layers

What happened
Canva launched Canva Craft, its first in-house image model, trained on the templates in its own library (hierarchy, grid, and typography). It returns the image to the editor as a layered design and will integrate with Gemini and ChatGPT via MCP.
Why it matters
It is a model trained on design structure, and the output stays editable. The stated reason was cost: serving AI with third-party models had become expensive, and the cost per task had already dropped 90% since April.

Relation to lab-design-systems Hierarchy, grid, and typography are the raw material of a design system.

Source

  • AiDrops, Oct 27: canva.com

No.10this month

Interfaces generated by the model and apps open to agents

What happened
OpenAI introduced Intelligent UI, in which GPT-6 answers with interactive interfaces instead of long text, and Apple will open MCP in macOS 27 so any agent can work in Pages, Keynote, and Numbers.
Why it matters
Both stories change UX work. The model starts designing the interface of the answer, and the native app starts exposing actions to agents.

Sources

  • AiDrops, Oct 8: openai.com
  • AiDrops, Oct 29: theverge.com
04

AI and civic activism

No.3this month

Cities stop cameras and data centers

What happened
In the US, more than 214 cities and counties have ended contracts with Flock Safety, which makes AI cameras that read license plates, since 2021 (90 in August 2026 alone), and in the second quarter 45 data center projects, totaling US$68 billion, were stalled by local opposition.
Why it matters
Local decisions still hold back AI infrastructure. After the backlash, Flock cut data retention from 30 to 7 days, and Matt Garman, of Amazon, announced that the company no longer uses NDAs with counties and pledged US$1 billion to host communities.

Sources

  • TechDrops, Oct 5: techcrunch.com
  • TechDrops, Oct 23: techdrops.com.br
  • AI Secret, Oct 5

No.4this month

Who pays for data that is out in the open

What happened
Meta changed the terms for business accounts so that, starting October 30, having an account authorizes scraping of the websites linked to the business, and after criticism it removed "training" and restricted the purposes to advertising and business.
Why it matters
Legal experts still see gaps between what Meta says and what the terms say. TechDrops lists three competing ways out: the Cloudflare toll, which blocks AI bots by default and created pay per crawl; licensing, as Reddit does with Google and OpenAI; and an open standard, a kind of robots.txt with a price list. For Longward, which aims to preserve a community's shared data, this is the underlying discussion.

Source

  • TechDrops, Oct 19: mercurynews.com

No.9this month

r/opensource bans AI projects

What happened
r/opensource, with about 390,000 members, banned projects that use AI, are made by AI, or are for AI, and within a day the post drew about 1,400 comments and a breakaway forum, r/TrueOpenSource.
Why it matters
The moderator said Linux would be accepted, and the Linux maintainers plan to drop the "Assisted-by" tag that marked AI help. For anyone publishing open source built with agentic processes, each community's rule on AI now matters when it is time to share the work.

Sources

  • AI Secret, Oct 26, and AiDrops, Oct 27: reddit.com
